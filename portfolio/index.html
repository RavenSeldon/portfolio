Independent Computational Research · 2026

Monitoring Under Scarcity

From information to causal use in resource-bounded intelligent systems

Research overview

A ground-truth computational study testing whether information present in an internal monitoring channel can be distinguished from information that actually influences behaviour.

E0-A uses exact enumeration, four quantitative acceptance criteria, eight adversarial controls, and 500 retained training initializations. The frozen experimental record and reproducibility archive are deposited on Zenodo.

Key result

An informative-but-unused control carried the same hidden-state information as a successful system while producing zero behavioural benefit, separating information availability from causal use operationally.

Current Research

How do resource-bounded intelligent systems decide which internal information gets to influence behaviour?

My current independent work focuses on the distinction between information availability and causal use. E0-A validates measurements for that distinction under known ground truth; the proposed E1 study asks what happens when several useful monitoring channels must compete for limited encoding or routing capacity.

I am particularly interested in computational bridges to attention, metacognitive control, information sampling, resource allocation, and human-centred intelligent systems.
